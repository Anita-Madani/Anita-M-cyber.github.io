Research

Iceberg Attention: Sublinear Long-Context Decoding With Mixture-of-Gaussians
Armin Gerami, Lasha Koroshinadze, Seyedehanita Madani, Prashant Pandey, Ramani Duraiswami
Preprint, 2026 — Under review, ICLR 2027
Iceberg Attention makes long-context decoding more efficient by computing attention exactly for a small set of relevant tokens and approximating the combined contribution of the remaining context. Its Mixture-of-Gaussians implementation summarizes groups of cached tokens with compact statistics, reducing memory access without retraining the model. Experiments evaluate both decoding speed and performance on retrieval, coding, and reasoning tasks.
Morphing Through Time: Diffusion-Based Bridging of Temporal Gaps for Robust Alignment in Change Detection
Anita Madani, Vishal M. Patel — WACV 2026
Remote sensing change detection often suffers from severe misalignment when image acquisitions are separated by large seasonal or multi-year gaps. This paper introduces a diffusion-based semantic morphing pipeline that synthesizes intermediate “bridging” frames between bi-temporal images, enabling robust, stepwise correspondence estimation. The generated morphs guide RoMa-based dense registration, followed by a lightweight U-Net that produces a high-fidelity warp preserving true structural changes. Experiments on LEVIR-CD, WHU-CD, and DSIFN-CD demonstrate consistent improvements in both registration accuracy and downstream change detection across multiple backbones, highlighting the generality and effectiveness of diffusion-assisted temporal alignment.
DiffRegCD: Integrated Scene Alignment and Change Detection Using Diffusion Features
Anita Madani et al. — WACV 2026
DiffRegCD leverages diffusion models to predict dense flow fields between bi-temporal satellite images and warps multi-scale features before change prediction. The approach yields improved alignment in complex urban scenes while preserving genuine appearance changes and hard boundary details.
On The Application of Linear Attention in Multimodal Transformers
Armin Gerami, Seyedehanita Madani, Ramani Duraiswami — CVPR Workshops 2026
This work investigates linear attention as an efficient alternative to softmax attention in multimodal transformers. Experiments with ViT models trained on LAION-400M show competitive zero-shot classification performance and similar scaling trends across model sizes. Attention-layer benchmarks demonstrate linear growth in runtime with sequence length, reducing the cost of processing long sequences.
